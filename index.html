    Climate outlook

    Sea level in New York Harbor has risen more than a foot since 1900, and the rise is speeding up. Higher seas mean today's "nuisance" high tides become regular flooding, and storm surges reach farther inland. Heavier downpours, like Ida's 3.15 in/hr in Central Park (2021), already exceed what most sewers can drain.

    About the data

      How the risk level is calculated

      Each place gets a coastal and a stormwater score. Live conditions (predicted tide plus current storm surge vs. NOAA flood thresholds; forecast rain intensity vs. NYC sewer capacity) are rated 0–4, then scaled by how exposed the place is (0–3). The higher of the two wins. Active NWS flood alerts and recent 311 flooding reports nearby raise the score.

      Neighborhood exposure ratings are simplified from FEMA flood maps and NYC DEP stormwater maps. Address checks ask FEMA for the actual flood zone, and use a year of 311 flooding reports as a stand-in for stormwater exposure.

      This is an informational tool, not an official warning. Follow Notify NYC and the National Weather Service in an emergency.
